# Forecasting guide

Reckonsolve is for calibrated observational judgment. Choose a question that interests you, is meaningfully uncertain, and can be resolved from a clear observation or source.

## Choose the right contract

| Situation | Mode |
| --- | --- |
| The outcome will become fixed and knowable later; you may update beliefs along the way. | Adaptive |
| The answer already exists, but you have not looked; you settle one final guess before checking. | One-Shot |
| The result mainly measures whether you follow through on a goal or can be steered to match your forecast. | Reconsider the question; it is not an ordinary observational forecast. |

## Before saving

Ask yourself:

- Am I mainly observing the outcome, or could my later choices materially steer it?
- Is there a clear target, source, and measurement convention for checking the answer?
- If I can influence it, have I fixed a realistic behavioral policy in Resolution Criteria before committing?
- For Adaptive, does the Deadline describe a meaningful forecasting window rather than a cutoff chosen for a preferred score?

A policy-conditioned question can be admissible when its intervention policy is objective, realistic, and covers the material channels of influence. A material breach makes the prediction Invalid. Real-world welfare takes precedence over preserving a score.

A One-Shot tree-height estimate or Yes/No probability about a hidden fact qualifies even though the answer exists. Settle the guess before revealing it. Phone notes are external records; entering them later does not make Reckonsolve a witness to the earlier commitment.

Descriptions are optional. It is good practice to choose the source or measurement convention before looking and record it in Background for a One-Shot or Resolution Criteria for Adaptive.

## Choose an Adaptive Deadline

The Forecast Deadline is permanent and marks the last moment a revision or Review may commit. Expected Resolution is separate, editable planning metadata. A shortcut such as 7 days is a convenient date choice, not advice about a suitable horizon.

Avoid picking a deadline solely to match the most likely answer date, adding a distant safety buffer, or shortening the window to improve the score. Choose the meaningful window before learning the answer. Exact effective resolution time governs Adaptive scoring; One-Shot reported times are documentation only.
